## Taxonomy decisions

Do not collapse near-duplicate labels automatically.

- `control-theory` and `control-systems` require question-level scope review before consolidation.
- `robotics` and `robotics-mechatronics` require the same review.
- `aerospace`, `aerodynamics`, and `orbital-mechanics` should remain distinct if their questions actually test different levels of abstraction.
- `electrical-engineering` and `mechanical-engineering` remain compatibility aliases rather than duplicate selectable topics.
- `engineering` and `string-theory` remain hidden compatibility topics until their historical content is explicitly re-homed.

## Vault boundary

Vault is the highest-priority boundary review because its historical question pack contains a mixture of:

- legitimate academic cryptography and cybersecurity history;
- declassified-program history;
- intelligence/UAP claims;
- speculative frontier-physics interpretations.

These should not be treated as a single evidentiary class. The next Vault cleanup should separate academic questions that belong in standard subjects from Vault-only material, without duplicating IDs or silently upgrading disputed claims into established facts.

## Provenance

The Question schema now supports optional `sources`, `reviewedAt`, and `factualAsOf` fields. Existing questions are not bulk-filled. Provenance should be added during real editorial review, especially for:

- SOTA material;
- current technology claims;
- historical/intelligence claims;
- contested scientific interpretations;
- quantitative claims that can change.

## Recommended augmentation sequence

Augmentation should proceed field by field after the quality gate is clean:

1. **Chemistry** — only 9 questions across the entire field. Raise Physical, Organic, and Inorganic Chemistry to at least 9 each.
2. **Biology** — all five topics are below the minimum viable target; strengthen Molecular Biology, Genetics, and Ecology first.
3. **Earth & Space** — strengthen Astronomy, Geology, Climate Science, then Meteorology.
4. **Computer Science foundations** — Algorithms and Machine Learning before adding new CS subfields.
5. **Finance foundations** — Microeconomics, Macroeconomics, Econometrics, Fixed Income, Portfolio Theory, and Algo Trading.
6. **Engineering** — a larger dedicated wave because 21 of 23 topics are thin.
7. **Human Sciences** — bring the three existing topics from 6 toward 9+ before adding new disciplines.
8. Mathematics and Physics expansion only after the thinner fields are healthier.

The goal is not equal question counts everywhere. It is enough independent material that a quiz can vary meaningfully and still represent the subject honestly.
